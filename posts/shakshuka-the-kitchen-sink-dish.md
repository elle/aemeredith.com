## Leadership is like shakshuka: why the best leaders use a "kitchen sink" strategy

In the high-pressure environment of a professional kitchen, a "kitchen sink" shakshuka is more than just a meal; it is a masterpiece of situational awareness. It begins with the non-negotiables: the fragrant onions, the punchy garlic, the rich tomato base, and obviously eggs. These represent your foundational leadership skills: authenticity, core values, clear communication, and emotional intelligence that allow you to relate and connect with your team.  But the magic happens in what you add next.

Great leadership, much like a complex recipe, requires a myriad of skills, character traits, and wisdom. It is rarely about following a static, black-and-white handbook. Instead, it is about the **"winning formula", **the ability to blend disparate ideas, individual talents, and sudden bursts of passion into a cohesive, successful outcome.

Daniel Goleman famously compared leadership styles to a set of **golf clubs**. You wouldn’t use a driver to sink a two-foot putt, nor would you use a putter to clear a water hazard. The Executive Chef understands this implicitly. They don't have just one "signature move"; they have a pantry full of responses.

As a leader, you are the **mastermind** and the **director**. You might need to be the "Pacesetter" when the heat is high and the deadline is looming, pushing the team when needed. Other times, you must be the "Coach," allowing the team to simmer to develop deep, long-term flavour and potential. The "Kitchen Sink" philosophy acknowledges that you pull in whatever you need in the moment, depending entirely on the context of the situation.

### Vision: the chef’s north star

Throwing everything into the pan without a plan results in chaos, not a masterpiece. This is where **vision** becomes the most critical ingredient. A Chef isn’t just a cook; they are a **creator** who holds the final image of the dish in their mind’s eye long before the heat is turned on.

Vision is the "recipe" that guides the leader into the future. It provides the "why" behind every addition to the pot. When a leader possesses a clear vision, the "kitchen sink" approach becomes a strategic advantage rather than a lack of focus. You aren't just reacting to the moment; you are curating it. You understand how the sharp acidity of a difficult conversation today will balance the richness of a shared victory tomorrow.

### The winning formula

> ***The Chef's Perspective:**** A leader doesn't just manage a kitchen; they balance the heat of the moment with the vision of the final plate. They know when to spice things up with a bold new strategy and when to let the base ingredients speak for themselves.*

Ultimately, to lead is to mix the raw abilities of your team with the knowledge and learned wisdom of your experience. By staying grounded in your "base ingredients", your core skills and values, while remaining flexible enough to add "spices" as the context demands, you create an environment where excellence is inevitable.

The Chef leader doesn’t just manage a kitchen; they cultivate an experience. They know that the perfect outcome isn't found in a set recipe, but in the skilful, visionary blending of every resource at their disposal. The best leaders understand that the "kitchen sink" approach isn't about chaos; it’s about **versatility**. By mixing a myriad of character traits and technical abilities, you create a winning formula that is more than the sum of its parts.
